Responsible AI means designing, building and using AI systems so they are fair, safe, transparent and accountable.

## Core principles

| Principle | In practice |
| --- | --- |
| Fairness | Test the system for different groups of users and fix unequal outcomes. |
| Safety | Prevent harmful output and limit what the system can do without oversight. |
| Privacy | Collect only the data you need and protect personal data in prompts, logs and training sets. |
| Transparency | Tell people when they are interacting with AI and explain what it can and cannot do. |
| Accountability | Name an owner for each AI system and keep records of how it was built and tested. |

## Before you launch

- Identify who could be affected and how the system could fail them.
- Review the training and source data for bias, errors and personal data.
- Evaluate the system on safety as well as quality. See [Evaluating AI systems](evaluating-ai-systems.md).
- Decide which decisions need a person to review them.
- Check the legal and regulatory requirements in each region where you operate.

## After you launch

- Monitor output and user feedback for new problems.
- Give users a clear way to report issues and to reach a person.
- Review the system regularly and whenever the model, data or use changes.

## Using AI to write documentation

- Have a subject-matter expert check generated content before it is published.
- Do not paste confidential or customer data into tools that are not approved for it.
- Follow your organisation's policy on disclosing AI-assisted content.
